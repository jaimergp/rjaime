This post is a chronological enumeration of acoustic guitarists that blew my mind the moment I saw them play. They have been instrumental (heh) in shaping up my understanding on what one can do with modern fingerstyle.

<!--more-->

## John Butler - Ocean

[📺 _John Butler Trio - Ocean (Live at Federation Square)_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LsnFvEQYJPU)

I remember not being able to close my mouth for the whole thing. A live take landed on my screen via an event shared on Tuenti, a Facebook-style social network that Spaniards used in the 2000s. Events were misused in all kinds of ways and one of them was sharing things you liked. If you liked it too, you would "attend" and hence join the comments section.

I don't remember who sent it to me or how it got there but I'm glad it did. I didn't know you could use tapping on acoustic guitars in such a musical way! Always assumed you needed the soft strings and the compression of an amp to pull (heh) the song out of the strings. But nope, there was John doing it on an eleven-string steel string acoustic. Yes, 11, he doesn't double the G. Through him I also discovered open tunings and the fact that you could place conveniently timed hits around the guitar body to add percussive elements. Mind. Blown.

I immediately tried to learn the whole piece on my Spanish guitar, which in hindsight was a relief: nylon is definitely more welcoming than steel to tender fingertips. My college friends were astounded when I showed them and one them drew this piece one evening because my interpretation of the beginning reminded him of hopping deers.

Eventually this led me to buying my first proper acoustic, with a hybrid pickup system, with the expectation of doing this sort of thing live one day. And I did! But that's a story for another post.

## Newton Faulkner - Teardrop

[📺 _Newton Faulkner teardrops live (Massive Attack cover)_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rjT86g9gTKk)

This cover of Massive Attack filmed live in a tiny elevator blew my mind. Too. I knew by then that you could tap and hit your guitar body with very musical results. And I assumed it was incredibly challenging to keep your tempo and balance the dynamics, so it had to be a skillset confined to instrumental pieces only, right? Well, when Newton started singing I was floored. Turns out that humans can do all those things at the same time just fine.

Then I saw him play _I need something_ and I fell in love with the piece. Somehow I ended up mixing the intro riff with the beginning of Ocean for fun and I like how the two of them mesh in that mashup I affectionately call Marejadilla.

## Jon Gomm - Passionflower

[📺 _Jon Gomm - Passionflower_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nY7GnAq6Znw)

When I thought it couldn't get more impressive, I stumbled upon this masterpiece. Jon, armed only with his guitar, starts with a mesmerisingly catchy melody that traverses the whole neck with tapping, harmonics, finger picking and... even adjusts the tuning pegs to raise or drop the pitch in precise half steps adding a captivating glide. He then continues and adds a percussive beat on top, far from the straight kick and snare. And because that was not enough... he starts singing! What the actual F! So skilled, talented and musical that it feels illegal. One of my favorite pieces to day.
